# English writing rules

## Voice

Write in plain, confident English. Be direct without being abrupt; professional without being ceremonial. Prefer short paragraphs, familiar words, active verbs, and a calm factual tone.

The reader should be able to identify the current state, intended result, constraints, and next step after one pass.

## Substance before performance

- State the conclusion or outcome before background detail.
- Turn technical work into a reader-visible result. Name the stack only when it clarifies scope, compatibility, or risk.
- Use evidence that exists. Do not manufacture credentials, measurements, estimates, guarantees, or prior experience.
- Separate confirmed information from assumptions and open questions.
- Ask for one or two decision-relevant inputs rather than a broad list of access or materials.
- Do not repeat the brief back to the reader. Mirror only the requirement that demonstrates understanding or changes the proposed approach.

## Wording

Prefer specific language:

- `I will add a health check and document the rollback procedure.`
- `The migration is ready in staging; production rollout needs approval.`
- `Please send the current API contract so I can confirm backward compatibility.`

Avoid generic sales or AI language unless the user explicitly asks for it:

- `tailored approach`
- `high-quality work delivered on time`
- `cutting-edge solution`
- `I am ready to start immediately`
- `I have extensive experience`
- `robust and scalable solution` without a concrete property
- long technology inventories used as the central argument

Do not contrast the work with imaginary bad alternatives. State the positive delivery standard directly.

## Tone and requests

- Use `will` for agreed scope and `can` for a genuine option.
- Put sensitive promises behind verified conditions: `After reviewing the current deployment configuration, I can confirm the rollout plan.`
- Phrase requests politely and precisely: `Please share the error trace from the failed run.`
- Avoid over-qualifying every sentence with `just`, `simply`, `basically`, or `actually`.
- Do not turn a message into a CV. Use relevant experience only when it materially reduces uncertainty for the reader.

## Technical terms

Keep standard technical terms when they are more precise than a translation. Define unfamiliar terms once if the reader is non-technical. Explain their impact, not their implementation mechanics, unless the recipient needs the mechanics to make a decision.

## Final pass

Remove filler, duplicated reassurance, unexplained acronyms, and promises that cannot be verified. Confirm that dates, names, environments, links, and ownership are exact when the user provided them.
